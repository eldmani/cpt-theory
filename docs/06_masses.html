The Key Observation: The Koide Formula

In 1982, Yoshio Koide noticed something extraordinary about the three charged lepton masses:

🎯 Simple version — the Koide formula

The electron, muon, and tau are three "copies" of the same particle, just with different masses. In the Standard Model, those masses (0.511 MeV, 105.66 MeV, 1776.86 MeV) are just measured and written down — no explanation why those specific values.

The Koide formula says: take the square roots of the three masses. Add them up. Square the sum. Divide the plain sum by the squared-root sum. You get exactly 2/3. Every time. To 0.01% precision. Like finding out that the lengths of three random guitar strings satisfy a perfect musical ratio — except it's the masses of fundamental particles. CPT derives why this ratio is 2/3: it's the geometry of an equilateral triangle in a circle.

(m_e + m_μ + m_τ) / (√m_e + √m_μ + √m_τ)² = 2/3 This holds to 0.01% precision — one of the most precise formulas in physics, yet unexplained in the Standard Model

Where Does θ₀ Come From?

The Koide formula involves one free angle θ₀. CPT derives this angle from the Weinberg angle (sin²θ_W), which itself comes from α = 1/137 alone.

🎯 Simple version — the chain from one number to masses

From α, you can derive sin²θ_W (the Weinberg angle — how much the electromagnetic and weak forces are mixed). From that, you get θ₀ (the Koide tilt angle). From θ₀, the Koide formula gives you the mass ratios. So the ratio m_τ/m_μ ≈ 16.8 ultimately traces back to 1/137.
