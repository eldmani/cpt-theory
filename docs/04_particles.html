🔄 Particles Are Stable Loops

Particles aren't tiny billiard balls. In CPT, they are stable periodic orbits — patterns that the configuration v keeps returning to, forever.

🎯 Simple version — what is a particle?

Drop a pebble in a pond. The ripples spread out and fade — the disturbance disappears. Now imagine a pool with a special geometry where the ripple bounces back and reinforces itself, over and over, never fading. That's a stable orbit — and in CPT, that's a particle. An electron isn't a tiny marble sitting somewhere. It's a self-reinforcing pattern of information that keeps recreating itself every cycle.

Most patterns fade away. Only a special few survive forever — those are the particles we see.

v₀ = T̂ · Ĉ(v₀) A state v₀ is "stable" if, after one complete propagation cycle (Ĉ, then T̂), you return to exactly v₀

This is called a fixed point. The universe's ground state is one giant fixed point. But there are also smaller fixed points — local patterns that are self-consistent. These are particles.

Watch how the propagation transforms a state. Most states drift away (unstable). A few states — the fixed points — return to themselves. These are the particles.

Period-3 Orbits: Why 3 Colors

Not all stable loops are the same period. A period-1 orbit returns after one step. A period-3 orbit visits three distinct states before returning. CPT identifies these with the three color charges of quarks.

🎯 Simple version — period-3 and color charge

Think of a traffic light: Red → Green → Yellow → Red → ... It cycles through exactly 3 states before returning to the start. That's a period-3 orbit. Quarks are like traffic lights — they cycle through 3 internal states (we call them Red, Green, Blue color charge). They can never be caught in just one state for long — always cycling. That's why quarks can never be isolated: you'd need to "freeze" the traffic light, which is impossible.

Period 1: Leptons

Return to same state after 1 step. No color charge. Examples: electron, muon, tau, neutrinos.

Period 2: Gauge Bosons

Visit 2 states alternately. Spin-1 structure. Examples: photon (U(1)), W/Z (SU(2)).

Period 3: Quarks

Visit 3 states in sequence. Exactly 3 color charges (R, G, B). The Z₃ orbit structure IS color charge.

The key insight: The period-3 orbit in the complex propagator has a Z₃ symmetry — rotating by 120° maps the orbit to itself. This Z₃ symmetry is exactly the color symmetry group of QCD. It's not assumed — it's forced by the period-3 structure.

Why SU(3): Cartan's Classification

The three color charges form a symmetry group — but which one? CPT derives this from the Cartan classification of Lie groups.

Period-3 orbit → center Z₃ (the symmetry that rotates 3 states)
Cartan: Only two simple Lie groups have center Z₃: SU(3) and E₆
SU(3) has fundamental representation dim = 3
E₆ has fundamental representation dim = 27

The orbit has 3 states → fundamental rep must have dim = 3 → SU(3) ✓ E₆ is excluded because you'd need a 27-dimensional fundamental rep — but we only have 3 color states

The 8 gluons live in the adjoint representation of SU(3) — the 8-dimensional space of "symmetry generators." Three quarks form the fundamental triplet (R, G, B). The geometry is completely determined by having exactly 3 color states.

Particles and Antiparticles

In CPT, an antiparticle is simply the same orbit traversed in the reverse direction. Since t() is bijective (two-way), you can run the orbit forwards (particle) or backwards (antiparticle). They annihilate because running forward and backward together is the same as not moving — returning to the fixed point v₀.

Left: particle orbit (clockwise). Right: antiparticle (counterclockwise). Together: they cancel — annihilation is just orbit + anti-orbit = no orbit.
