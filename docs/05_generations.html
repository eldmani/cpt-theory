Why not a 4th family? Why not just 2? This is one of the deepest unanswered questions in physics. CPT has an answer.

The Sandwich Proof: N_g = 3

We prove N_g = 3 by squeezing it between a lower bound and an upper bound:

🎯 Simple version — the sandwich argument

Imagine you want to know how many wheels a car has. You know: at least 3 (because a 2-wheeled car falls over — that's the lower bound) and at most 3 (because a 4-wheeled car is too heavy for the engine — that's the upper bound). So it must be exactly 3.

For particle generations: with fewer than 3, you can't have CP violation (the "arrow of time" in particle physics). With more than 3, the Higgs field becomes unstable and the universe collapses. So it must be exactly 3.

⬇️

Lower Bound Step 1: CPT forces time's arrow

The resolution front moves one way — once a configuration is resolved (observed), it cannot be un-resolved. This is built into the CPT axiom that t() is bijective and resolution is irreversible.

↕️

Lower Bound Step 2: T-violation means CP violation

The CPT theorem (proved from Lorentz symmetry + unitarity) says: CPT is conserved. If T (time reversal) is violated, then CP must also be violated. Since T is violated by the irreversible resolution, CP violation must occur somewhere.

🧮

Lower Bound Step 3: The Jarlskog Invariant

The Koide orbit structure (explained below) produces a specific mathematical quantity called the Jarlskog invariant J. We can calculate it directly from the orbit angle θ₀:

J = 2√2 · sin(3θ₀) / Δ_d

where θ₀ = arcsin(sin²θ_W) ≈ 0.222 rad
sin(3 × 0.222) ≈ 0.619 ≠ 0 J ≠ 0 means CP violation is present in the theory — this is a concrete mathematical calculation, not an assumption

The orbit triangle rotates with angle θ₀. The CP phase = 3θ₀. When θ₀ ≠ 0 (which it isn't, since sin²θ_W ≠ 0), CP is violated.

📐

Lower Bound Step 4: Kobayashi-Maskawa Theorem

The KM theorem is an established mathematical result: CP violation in quark mixing requires at least 3 generations. With only 2 generations, any CP-violating phase can always be removed by field redefinitions — it's not physical. With 3, one phase is "irremovable." Since J ≠ 0 (physical CP violation), we need N_g ≥ 3.

💥

Upper Bound: N_g = 4 Destroys the Higgs

If there were a 4th generation, the Z₄ Koide formula (the generalized version of the Koide formula) predicts the 4th-generation top-like quark would have mass ≈ 493 GeV. This gives it Yukawa coupling y ≈ 2.84 — much larger than 1. This destabilizes the Higgs field.

The Higgs quartic coupling λ runs with energy. For N_g=3 (Standard Model), it just barely stays positive — the universe barely holds together. For N_g=4, λ goes negative immediately — no electroweak symmetry breaking, no masses, no physics.

The Final Result

N_g ≥ 3 AND N_g ≤ 3 → N_g = 3

Three particle generations is not an accident. It's the unique number that:

  • Is large enough for physical CP violation (→ time's arrow, matter-antimatter asymmetry)
  • Is small enough that the Higgs field remains stable (the universe holds together)
  • Matches the Koide orbit structure exactly (period-3 orbit = Z₃ symmetry = 3 colors = 3 generations)
