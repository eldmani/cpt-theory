What Is Dark Energy?

In 1998, astronomers discovered that the universe isn't just expanding — it's accelerating. Something is pushing galaxies apart faster and faster. This "something" is called dark energy, and it makes up ~68% of the total energy of the universe.

🎯 Simple version — the cosmological constant problem

Quantum mechanics predicts that empty space should be seething with energy from virtual particles popping in and out. When you calculate how much energy, you get a number 10¹²° times bigger than what we observe. That's a 1 followed by 120 zeros. It's the worst prediction in physics.

CPT's answer: most of that quantum energy is in "modes" (vibration patterns) that are beyond the Hubble horizon — the edge of the observable universe. The resolution front hasn't reached them yet, so they haven't been "resolved" into physical reality. They're still quantum, not classical. Only the small fraction of modes inside the horizon contribute to dark energy — and that fraction is tiny, matching the observed value.

In the Standard Model of Cosmology (ΛCDM), dark energy is just a constant — the cosmological constant Λ — added by hand. Its value is measured but not explained.
